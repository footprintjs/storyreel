# The stage: places, props and actions as building blocks

*Design, 2026-10-27. Code is cited as file · symbol under `storyreel/src/`. Built in `src/stage/`, published as
`footprint-storyreel/stage`. All example content is invented and neutral.*

## 1. Why

A children's cartoon series made with StoryReel costs about 500k AI tokens an episode. Almost all of it goes on new
drawing code: every new place (a shop, a kitchen, a walk home, a storehouse) is a new world module of 150 to 650
lines, and every new action (filling a bag, handing a thing over, opening a door, counting things out) is written
again inside it. Two of those modules, a shop (193 lines) and a walk home (150 lines; both counted by hand in the series' own
repository), show the pattern. Each one
again does:

- a backdrop;
- people placed and walking (`walk.mjs · walk`);
- props appearing (a bag that pops onto a table);
- things hopping into a container, one per beat, with a count badge;
- things handed over (from a bag to a hand, then to someone else);
- doors opening;
- the timing of each of these (arcs, anticipation, landing), its refusals, its camera names and its sounds.

Only the drawings differ between worlds. The rest is the same choreography, written again by hand each time.

The stage makes the choreography data. A scene is a **place**, a **cast**, **props** and **actions** from a
small fixed vocabulary. The library owns the timing, the refusals, the camera names and the continuity record.
The drawings come through three adapters. A new scene becomes a few lines of JSON, and a new place
becomes one drawing that every later scene reuses.

## 2. The data

One stage is one place. A recipe gives it where any story-kit world goes: the story, or a `world` stage.

```json
{"kit": "demo", "place": "shop",
 "cast":  {"shopkeeper": {"as": "adult", "x": 1250, "facing": "left"}, "customer": {"as": "child", "x": 420}},
 "props": {"crate":  {"kind": "crate", "at": "counter"},
           "bag":    {"kind": "bag", "at": "table"},
           "apples": {"kind": "apple", "count": 10, "in": "crate"}},
 "actions": [
   {"do": "count", "what": "apples", "into": "bag", "who": "shopkeeper", "at": [["shop", "{a1}"], ["shop", "{a2}"]]},
   {"do": "walkTo", "who": "customer", "to": "bag", "at": ["shop", "{full}"]},
   {"do": "pickUp", "who": "customer", "what": "bag", "at": ["shop", "{take}"]},
   {"do": "exit", "who": "customer", "at": ["shop", "{bye}"]}]}
```

- **place**: a name the kit's place adapter has. The place gives the floor's y, named spots (`"at": "table"`)
  and named regions (a sign, the shelves).
- **cast**: `id → {as?, x, facing?}`. `as` names the person drawing (the id by default); `x` is where they stand
  on the floor; `facing` is `left` or `right` (default: toward the middle). Someone whose first action is
  `enter` starts off stage.
- **props**: `id → {kind, count?, at | in | with, slots?, open?}`. A prop is one thing or a group of `count`
  things of one kind. It sits at a place (`at`: `[x, y]` or a spot), in a container (`in`), or in someone's
  hands (`with`). A container is a prop whose kind has slots, for example a bag, a crate or a box. `slots`
  overrides how many slots it has. `open` is the starting state of a door or lid.
- **actions**: listed in the order they start. Every time is a beat, as everywhere else in StoryReel (Law 2).
- `aside: true` marks a stage outside the story's timeline, such as a flash-forward, a memory or a teaser. Its
  record is kept, but its facts are not carried to the next shot.
- `sounds: false` silences the stage's own sounds.

## 3. The action vocabulary

| action | keys | what changes |
|---|---|---|
| `walkTo` | `who` (one or a list, keeping formation), `to` (x, a spot, a prop or a person), `at`, `style?` | where they stand (`walk.mjs · walk`) |
| `enter` / `exit` | `who` or `what`, `at`, `from?` / `to?` (`left`, `right`), `style?` | a person walks on or off; a prop pops in or out (`moves.mjs` · `pop`, `pop-out`) |
| `pickUp` | `who`, `what`, `n?`, `at`, `travel?` | a thing on the ground goes to their hands |
| `give` | `from`, `to`, `what`, `n?`, `at`, `travel?` | hands to hands |
| `putInto` | `who`, `what`, `n?`, `into`, `at`, `travel?` | hands to a container |
| `takeOutOf` | `who`, `what`, `n?`, `from`, `at`, `travel?` | a container to hands |
| `count` | `what`, `into`, `at: [beats]`, `from?`, `who?`, `badge?`, `travel?` | one thing a beat hops into the container (one event a beat); a badge counts |
| `fade` | `what`, `n?`, `from?`, `at` | n things are gone (eaten, used up) |
| `point` | `who`, `on`, `at`, `until?` | an arm toward a thing |
| `open` / `close` | `what`, `who?`, `at` | a door or lid swings |
| `lookAt` | `who` (one or a list), `on` (or `viewer`), `at` | where the eyes go |
| `countOnFingers` | `who`, `at`, `up`, `fold?` or `raise?` (beats), `until?` | fingers fold or rise on the beats (`fingers.mjs · fingersAt`) |

A thing that moves between holders travels by a **strategy** (`TRAVELS`): `hop` (count), `toss` (give), `lift`
(pickUp, takeOutOf), `drop` (putInto), or `slide`. A count's badge is one too (`BADGES`): `total` (one number over
the container), `each` (a number over each thing), or `none`.

## 4. What the library owns

- **Timing and motion, as pure functions of time.** Each strategy is an arc (`principles.mjs · arc`) eased over
  its seconds. A thing winds back before it goes (`anticipate`) and squashes as it lands, then settles
  (`squash`). Arms and doors share one shape: wind back, swing, overshoot, settle (the shape `fingers.mjs`
  already uses). Walks use `walk.mjs`, fingers use `fingersAt`, and props popping in or out use `moves.mjs`. All
  of the numbers are in `STAGE` and `TRAVELS`. The swing is one shape, `swing.mjs`, shared with `fingersAt`. An arm
  or a pair of hands that starts while the last is still coming down carries on from where it is (no second
  wind-back), and turns to its new target over `STAGE.look`, so nothing jumps between frames. A prop's presence is
  a list, as a person's is, so a prop may go and come back.
- **Events in time order.** Each action is one event on its beat; a count is one event a beat, and a count on fingers
  one as the hands come up and one per fold or raise. Events run in time order, list order breaking ties, so every
  check and every ledger entry sees the story as it stands at that moment. A container's slot is taken as a thing
  sets off toward it, so a thing on its way already counts where it is going.
- **Refusals, checked when the film compiles,** each naming the action and the fix. They cover: giving or putting
  away a thing you do not hold; taking more out of a container than it holds; an 11th thing into 10 slots; a
  closed lid; a container that is still filling when it is picked up; someone out of reach; one person doing two
  things at once (a look excepted); someone off stage; an unknown verb, key, id, place, person or prop kind (the
  last three list what the kit has); actions out of time order; and an id named like a property every JavaScript
  object has (`__proto__`, `constructor`, `prototype`). Names a recipe writes are looked up among own keys only, and
  every record keyed by them is built from Maps (`own.mjs`), so no name reaches `Object.prototype`.
- **Names for the camera.** `regionsAt(t)` names each person, each prop (a group by its id: the box around all its
  things), and the place's own regions. A push or a camera move frames them by name. Two things with one name
  refuse.
- **A continuity record.** After every event, the ledger says who holds what, how many are where (a thing on its way
  counts where it is going), and who is on stage. It also gives a start and end list of facts (`"apples in bag": 10`, `"bag": "with customer"`). The film
  reads both from the world (`continuity`, `ledger`). They are joined with the shot's declared continuity (a
  disagreement refuses) and carried across shots by `shots.mjs · checkContinuity`. So a park stage that starts
  with 9 apples in the bag, after a shop that ended with 10, is refused by name. The ledger reaches
  `film.shots[i].ledger` and the making-of record.

## 5. The adapter interface (what the drawings supply)

```js
stageDrawings({name, places: {shop: {floor, spots?, regions?, draw(ctx, t), front?(ctx, t)}},
               people: {adult: {size, reach?, hand?, mouth?, draw(ctx, pose)}},
               props:  {bag: {size: [w, h], slots?, slotAt?, opens?, draw(ctx, look), front?(ctx, look)}},
               badge?(ctx, {x, y, n, pop, done})})
```

The drawings draw; the library never does, except a plain default badge. `stageDrawings` returns a kit a film takes
(`kits: [...]`): the drawings a stage uses, not a stage kit in the film's sense (one that adds a stage type). A person's `pose` carries everything the
drawing needs: `{x, y, facing, walk, arm, look, fingers, holding, talk, t}`. A prop is drawn with its bottom
centre at (0, 0); the library places, scales and fades it, and its `look` carries `{x, y, sx, sy, scale, opacity,
open, count, t}`. An unknown place, person or prop kind refuses, listing what the kit has. `readAdapters` checks
the shape once.

## 6. What stays in kits

- Every drawing and the look: the ink, the boil, paper grain, faces, clothes, idle life, moods.
- Faces and hands as camera names. The stage names whole people and props.
- Anything outside the vocabulary: a thought cloud, a time-travel bubble, a craft being made. A world kit still
  works as before. The stage is one more story kit, not a replacement.
- A world's own camera. The film's focus and camera moves frame the stage's names instead.

## 7. Not in this version

- Depth order beyond one rule: place, ground props, people, flight, badges, then the place's `front`.
- Reach between two people for `give` (a toss crosses the stage).
- A `putDown` onto the ground, and props that move by themselves.
- Speech-driven looks (`clock.gaze`). `pose.talk` is given, but a kit reads gaze itself.
